Log panel

The repository's event log. Every write goes through it — one revision per change, each holding the individual operations and their before/after snapshots — so the whole history is reviewable and reversible.

Browsing

Move through revisions with ↑ or k / ↓ or j; Enter expands a revision to show its operations, and the same keys then walk through those — the operation under the cursor is what a revert aims at. Watcher, reconcile and manual edits all appear here.

Reading the badges

The status column says what each revision is, which is what decides whether undoing it makes sense:

  • watcher — the daemon wrote it for the filesystem (a file arrived, moved, went away). Not yours to undo: rewinding past it would unrecord something that is really true on disk. Those rows are dimmed.
  • undo of #N — the revision is itself a revert of #N.
  • undone by #N — a later revert already put it back; its number is struck through.
  • HEAD — where the history currently stands.

Undo

Ctrl+z (log:undo) undoes the newest change you made, and picks the mechanism for it: a rollback when your change is the last thing in the log (HEAD simply moves back over it, leaving no trace), a revert when the watcher has written since (your change is undone in place and the watcher's revisions stay standing). Pressing it again walks further back through your own changes rather than undoing the undo.

Redo

Ctrl+Shift+z (log:redo) is the mirror: it takes the newest undo back. HEAD moves forward again when the undo was a rollback; when the watcher has written in between — a file moved after you pressed Ctrl+z — the undo is itself undone in place, so the redo survives it instead of refusing. When the newest thing you wrote is an ordinary change rather than an undo, there is nothing to redo: redo takes an undo back, it does not re-apply arbitrary history (that is log:rollback).

On the command line the same two decisions are mf log undo and mf log redo; add plan to either to see what it would do without writing.

Undoing one past change: revert

A revert writes the inverse of a past revision as a new revision at HEAD: nothing leaves the history, and everything written since stays where it is. Shift+R (log:revert) reverts whatever is selected — the revision, or a single operation of it when the cursor is on one. Shift+O narrows to one operation from a revision row, and Shift+V widens back to the whole revision.

A revert can be blocked: a later operation overwrote the same cell, so undoing this one alone would mean nothing. The panel then selects the blocker and says so — revert that one first, or take both together with Shift+D (log:revert-with-dependents), which reverts the target and everything in its way. When the revert needs to move files on disk, the panel hands you the mf log revert line to run instead, so the moves stay coordinated with the metadata.

The rest

log:rollback moves HEAD to the selected revision (a rollback, or a redo forward). Shift+L labels a revision so you can come back to it by name, and Shift+G draws every branch instead of the active line. log:prune permanently removes history before the selected revision.

Beyond the panel

The CLI exposes the rest: mf log list to read history, mf log rollback to move HEAD (with a coordinated file-move policy), mf log revert for a coordinated revert, and mf log prune to compact old history. A rollback that would overwrite a file on disk routes the loser to the trash first.
