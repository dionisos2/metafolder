* Storage: what is left after the dense id work
The invariant "a record keeps its dense id between two reindexes" is built (doc
"Deleting a metarecord in the store"). What is left:

- Trigrams kept by mode per (field, trigram) rather than per field (user
  decision, 23 November 2026; to build next, then measure). Why: the bench's
  =query.subtree_range_text= (a subtree, =rating > 4=, =file1= on 2 000 short
  names) reads 2 863 keys counted and 2 701 for a page, against 626 and 625 at
  =2c8bb168=, where every field kept its trigrams by metarecord. The names stay
  by value (the switch looks at the field's mean, texts per trigram, about 13
  against 16, while =fil=, =ile=, =txt= are in every name), and by value the
  trigrams name texts, which cannot be cut to the 380 candidates of the subtree
  and the range: the join of =fil=, =ile=, =le1= seeks three runs per text they
  share (1 111 of 2 100), then reads each text's posting. 6d561539 only bounds
  that join (doc "Text search in the store").
  - A trigram's key says its mode: =field · trigram · 0= holds the set of the
    texts holding it (one value, read once: a rare trigram's list is short, so
    rewriting it when a new text comes is cheap), =field · trigram · 1= its
    holders as chunked bitmaps of ids (no union of postings to read, which is
    what a common trigram costs by value). The per-entry keys of
    =index_trigram_texts= and the per-field mode go.
  - A trigram turns by metarecord when its texts get too many (a threshold per
    trigram, to choose and measure): the next fold builds its bitmap from the
    union of its texts' postings, once. It never turns back. A write adds its id
    to the field's pending set, as today, and a fold builds the bitmaps of the
    trigrams by metarecord for the pending ids.
  - A search: the intersection of its trigrams by metarecord gives a set of ids
    M (plus the pending ids, unknown), which cuts the candidates C at once; the
    intersection of its trigrams by value gives a set of texts V, whose sizes are
    known before anything else is read. The texts of V are matched against the
    pattern on the text itself, before any posting is read. Then the cheaper
    order: read the postings of the texts of V that match and intersect with
    M ∩ C, or check the records of M ∩ C on their own rows (weighed with
    =SCATTERED=, doc "Query planner"). All trigrams in one mode is one of the two
    modes of today.
  - Stale entries stay allowed (a candidate too many, never one too few), so the
    sizes are upper bounds. A persisted format change: the conversion rewrites
    every field's trigrams (doc "Persisted format changes"), and =mf repo check=
    learns the two key forms.
  - Done when =tests/kv_cost.rs= holds the bench's query to about what =2c8bb168=
    read, =kv_text.rs= and the oracle pass, and the cost of a write of new names
    is measured against today's.
- Open questions, none decided (doc "Storage open questions"): replace and rename
  not being mass writes, the marks a deleted record loses, compressing what the log
  keeps of a deletion.
- The =undo_redo_properties= suite takes 2 min 50 s on four cores (4 min 20 s
  before 23 November 2026: one query for a snapshot instead of a request per
  record, the log's head alone, the templates built once per run); what is left
  is the cases themselves: copying and loading a repository, the writes, the
  reverts, the folds and the checks.
- The sync store (=sync.rs=, one LMDB per pair of repositories: =meta=, =links=,
  =by_a=, =by_b=, =snaps=) was left out of the review of the store's tables
  (user decision, 20 November 2026): independent, with a migration of its own. To
  go over later on the same principles: compact encoding, snapshot fields in the
  record value format rather than JSON, the tables' names.
- A trace of the values a retype loses (user decision, 20 November 2026, to do
  later): with the store's table review, a value a retype cannot convert is
  removed rather than turned into =nothing=, and the field goes when none
  converts. The retype's summary says so and the log can undo it, but nothing
  finds those metarecords afterwards. Proposed: a multi-valued user field, say
  =retype_lost=, one text per lost value (=<field> = <original value>=), without a
  prefix so that it is cleared without =--force=, as =orphan= is (doc "Orphans");
  =mfr_retype_lost= was the alternative.
- Strings of any bytes (user wish, to decide later): a string should be able to
  hold whatever a =tree_ref= name holds, bytes that need not be UTF-8. The store
  will be ready (the table review encodes a string as =varint len · bytes=), but
  the model is not: =Value::String= is a Rust =String= (=core/metarecord.rs=), and
  so are the wire, the queries and the trigrams; doc "Changing a field's type"
  turns a name that is not UTF-8 into its displayed form (=%XX=). The wire would
  carry such a string as paths are carried, ={text, bytes}= (doc "Tree
  names").
- The bench's history is stale: =2c8bb168= measured today is 4 to 12 times slower
  than its own baseline of 9 November on every =query.*= scenario (=query.count=
  0.77 ms against 0.14), so the bench's verdicts against it mean nothing until a
  new baseline is recorded on a neutral machine (doc "The timed regression
  suite"; ask before).
- The mass-write threshold (=mass-write-threshold=, 20 to start) is one number for
  every operation: a way to derive better values from the repository is to come
  (doc "Mass writes in the store").
